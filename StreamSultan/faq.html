Getting Started

Everything you need for your first stream.

Stream Sultan is a simple browser-based screen sharing service. It lets you share your screen with other people through a real-time peer-to-peer connection. You create a stream, receive a viewer link, and share that link with your friends or audience.
No. Stream Sultan does not require an account, username, password, registration or login. The goal is to make starting a temporary screen-sharing session as quick as possible.
Open the Start Streaming page, configure your stream settings, choose what you want to share when your browser asks for permission, and start the session. Stream Sultan will generate a viewer link that you can share.
Simply send them the viewer link generated when you create the stream. They can open that link in a supported browser and connect to the session without creating an account.
Yes. A stream can be viewed by multiple connected viewers. Because Stream Sultan uses peer-to-peer WebRTC connections, actual performance can depend on the broadcaster's upload connection, the viewers' connections, browser capabilities and the number of active connections.

Streaming

How screen sharing and connections work.

Stream Sultan is designed primarily for screen sharing. Depending on the browser and the permission options presented during screen capture, you can generally select a screen, application window or browser tab.

Quality & Audio

Resolution, FPS, bandwidth and sound.

Stream Sultan is designed to support multiple screen-sharing quality levels, including 720p, 1080p and 4K. The actual quality you receive can depend on the selected source, browser, hardware, network conditions and available bandwidth.
Yes. The service is designed around 30 FPS and 60 FPS streaming options. Higher frame rates can require significantly more processing power and bandwidth, especially at higher resolutions.
Video quality can be affected by network bandwidth, congestion, browser behavior, hardware performance and the selected resolution or frame rate. If the connection cannot consistently sustain the requested quality, the effective stream quality may decrease.
Stream Sultan uses real-time WebRTC communication, but network conditions can still introduce latency. Distance, routing, congestion, Wi-Fi quality and system performance can all affect the delay between the broadcaster and viewer.
First check whether audio sharing was enabled when the screen capture was started. Some browsers and operating systems only provide system-audio capture for particular sharing sources. Also check that the viewer's browser is not muted and that the selected source actually provides audio.

Privacy & Security

What happens to your stream and connection.

Stream Sultan is designed around peer-to-peer WebRTC communication. The service itself is not designed as a conventional video-hosting platform where your screen recording is uploaded into a permanent video library. Technical infrastructure may still be involved in establishing and maintaining connections.
No built-in permanent stream archive is provided. Stream Sultan is intended for live, temporary screen sharing rather than permanent video storage.
Treat your generated viewer link as a private invitation. Anyone who obtains the link may be able to attempt to connect to the associated session. For that reason, only share the link with people you trust and avoid posting private stream links publicly.
There is no account creation flow requiring a name, username or password. However, browsers, networks and third-party infrastructure involved in delivering the service can process technical information as part of normal internet communication.
Stream Sultan uses standard web technologies and WebRTC for real-time media communication. PeerJS is used to simplify peer connection management. The browser handles the actual screen capture and media permissions.

Troubleshooting

Quick fixes for common connection problems.

Make sure the broadcaster's sender page is still open and the session is active. Confirm that the viewer is using the complete generated link. If the broadcaster's connection was interrupted, refreshing the sender page and creating a new session may resolve the issue.
A stream can be interrupted by a lost network connection, browser issues, the capture permission being revoked, the sender page being closed, or other WebRTC connection problems. Reconnecting or creating a new session can restore the stream.
Try refreshing the viewer page and reconnecting to the session. The broadcaster can also stop and restart screen sharing. If the problem continues, restarting the sender session can establish a fresh WebRTC connection.
Screen capture is controlled by your browser and operating system. Check whether screen-sharing permissions were denied, make sure you are using a supported browser, and try starting the capture again. Browser restrictions may also prevent capture in certain contexts.
If reconnecting does not work, the fastest solution is often to close the current session, reopen the sender page and create a new stream. A new session produces a new viewer link and a fresh connection state.
